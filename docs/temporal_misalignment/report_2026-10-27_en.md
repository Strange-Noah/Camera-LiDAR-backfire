## 1. Experiment Definition (T1: stale image + current alignment)

Per the group specification (Experiment T, finalized in the team math doc), T1 is defined as:

- Replace the 6 camera images with **real historical frames k steps back** along the `sample_data.prev` chain (k ∈ {1, 2});
- **Keep the current sample's calibration, poses, LiDAR point cloud, and GT unchanged** (i.e., the Section 16 dispute is resolved in favor of "current alignment," as finalized in the spec);
- LiDAR input is untouched — only the camera images are temporally misaligned.

**Measured misalignment delays** (from the substitution table `sub_table.json`, n = 5,869 samples × 6 cameras = 35,214 entries):

| Setting | Actual delay (current frame timestamp − historical frame timestamp) |
|---|---|
| k=1 | mean 77.3 ms, median 100 ms, range 43–252 ms |
| k=2 | mean 168.4 ms, median 150 ms, range 140–400 ms |

**Eligible set**: of the 6,019 val samples, 150 are scene-first frames with no predecessor and are naturally ineligible; **the eligible sets for k=1 and k=2 are identical: 5,869**. All comparisons below are paired on this same 5,869-sample subset.

## 5. Conclusions

1. **Temporal misalignment monotonically degrades fusion performance**: mAP drops with misalignment magnitude (clean 0.6855 → k1 0.6810 → k2 0.6699), NDS drops in step, and localization error mATE rises monotonically (0.2786 → 0.2828 → 0.2881) — consistent with stale images breaking geometric projection alignment.
2. **No backfire is observed within this range**: even at k=2 (mean 168 ms misalignment), fusion mAP 0.6699 remains clearly above LiDAR-only on the same subset (0.6479, **+2.2 pt**), and above the full-val LiDAR-only baseline (0.6443). The degradation is graceful — there is no crossover where "wrong images" become worse than "no images."
3. mASE/mAVE/mAAE are essentially unchanged — misalignment mainly hurts positional accuracy (mATE/mAOE), as expected.
4. Caveat: k ∈ {1,2} (≈0.08–0.17 s) is mild misalignment. Whether the backfire criterion triggers at larger misalignment requires larger k (e.g., k ∈ {3,5,10}) — a natural next step to discuss at the team meeting.
